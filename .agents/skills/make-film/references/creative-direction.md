# Direct the film before dispatching models

## Brief → dramatic intention → cut

Start with what the viewer should feel and understand, not a catalogue of visual adjectives. Specify an audience and viewing context: a quiet desktop short can hold on detail longer than a muted phone feed. Identify a protagonist's immediate want, an obstacle or surprising change, and the final emotional difference. For a tiny film, one readable turn is enough. Avoid compressing a feature-film synopsis into six seconds.

The executable example, **A Little Shelter**, is an original silent miniature for a general audience: a dry yellow leaf catches beside a tiny green seedling in rain, briefly threatens to flatten it, then settles as a shelter. Isolation → apprehension → relief. One seedling, one leaf, one continuous patch of ground, no human likenesses, logos, dialogue or third-party song. The viewer must see the change in rain exposure; a beautiful but unreadable macro photograph is not the payoff.

Before prompts, write a shot table with: dramatic purpose; subject/action; lens/framing; camera movement; beginning and ending state; screen direction; duration/handles; exact audible content; required anchors. Put the strongest change on screen. If a shot contributes neither orientation, new action, reaction nor payoff, cut or combine it.

## Coverage grammar that edits

- **Establish geography** with a wide or readable medium before spatially ambiguous detail. For the example, establish the seedling at frame right and the approaching leaf from frame left.
- **Motivate scale changes:** medium for the shared action, close-up for the consequence, detail for a causal prop. A row of unrelated wide/close/wide images is not coverage.
- **Maintain the action axis.** Choose a 180-degree line between subjects or along travel; keep cameras on the same side until an establishing/neutral/on-axis move explains a crossing. Preserve left-to-right travel, eyeline height, and eye space in reverse angles. Do not flip reference images just to repair a prompt.
- **Cut on an action only once.** The next shot picks up at the already-reached state, with an overlap handle available for the editor. It must not replay a hand picking up the same cup, a door opening again, or the example leaf arriving a second time.
- **Coverage is insurance, not compulsory extra generation.** A reaction or insert can hide a damaged join, but spend on it only if the current material cannot be trimmed into the intended beat. Prefer a usable single shot to three weak angles.
- Record prop hand, handedness, seating, gaze target, lighting direction, weather, wetness, wardrobe closure and object positions. For nonhuman subjects lock leaf shape/color and the number/orientation of seedling leaves. Check these in pixels across takes.

## Lock identity, state and voice separately

Maintain a cast bible: immutable identity traits; approved canonical reference; scene wardrobe/pose/state; forbidden changes; voice identity and consent. A different jacket is a state variant, not a new face. A room image containing people can be setting-only, not a new identity source. An approved scene reference does not automatically belong in every shot.

Library membership is not casting. Set visible shots and heard shots independently for each window. Specify scene/phone/offscreen/voiceover delivery; confirm speaker ownership and actual bound audio. Keep offscreen voices offscreen unless the story says otherwise. Do not assign a speaking role merely because a person is visible. More voice identities than a task supports requires an explicit staging/audio decision, not deleting a speaker or sharing one voice silently.

For dialogue, preserve literal words and punctuation as the approved script, in order. State who says each line, to whom, vocal intention, and pauses. Keep pronunciation notes out of the spoken quotation. No ad-libbing, paraphrase, extra greetings or repeated last lines unless approved. Estimate breath and response time aloud; then listen to the generated performance. A word-count estimate or aligned script is not evidence of intelligible audio.

## Manageable temporal units

Use the existing timing advisor/render plan instead of guessing task frame counts. Director timing is 24 fps and generation uses a 17k+5 grid; overlap and retained frames are not interchangeable. Default windows target 15 seconds, but short controlled beats are better calibration units. Do not enable 30-second spans merely to fit too many events. A model-specific limit still applies.

For each shot list **start state → one primary action → end state**. Give the viewer orientation and a held reaction; allow edit handles instead of forcing action on the first/last frame. Distinguish task generation span from the editorial duration after trims. Linked continuation needs compatible entry/exit and preserved context; an independent alternative cannot be assumed to preserve downstream continuity.

## Prompts are stage directions, not a synopsis

A useful hierarchy is: locked look/location → shot framing and axis → exact start state → one physical action → end state → explicit audio. Keep identity traits in anchors and only reinforce discriminating details. Prefer a single camera instruction. Do not ask simultaneously for locked-off framing, a dolly, a drone orbit and a handheld close-up.

Example shot intention (author through the public window schema; let the compiler supply reference numbering):

> Locked macro medium view at seedling height, overcast soft daylight, damp dark soil. The seedling stays frame right, its two green leaves angled upward. A single dry yellow oval leaf is already touching the ground at frame left. One gust tips it slowly toward the seedling; it settles above the stem without crushing it. Hold on the sheltered seedling. The camera stays still. Rain and a brief soft leaf rustle; no speech, singing or music.

For the following shot start with **“The yellow leaf is already settled as a shelter”**, not “A leaf blows in.” Describe visible behavior rather than “it feels hopeful.” Avoid contradictory negatives and ornate prose competing with the action. Read the compiled prompt, not only your input: stale references, duplicate Shot markers, contradictory wardrobe, and accidentally repeated dialogue are production errors.

## Image-first look development

Agree the palette, contrast, texture, lighting, lens distance and composition in a few inspected stills before video. Produce a contact sheet only if it helps compare distinct options; don't flood the input pool. Approve one clear identity anchor, then derive pose/wardrobe/location variants from it. Check anatomy, count, silhouette, materials and background; a model's self-rating does not establish consistency.

Calibrate one short shot at modest discovered settings. Review real motion, framing, identity, action completeness, unwanted speech and temporal stability. Change one causal variable per alternative where practical. Track what changed and why. Batch the remaining film only after the shot proves the chosen look/model/timing is usable within the authorized budget.

## Review rubric, not a quality guarantee

For each actual take mark **pass / repairable in edit / needs alternate / uncertain** for:

1. Story legibility without explanation.
2. Identity, wardrobe, props and geography.
3. Action progression with no resets/repeats/teleports.
4. Focus/exposure/texture, motion artifacts and temporal stability.
5. Exact words, voice ownership, sync and clean boundaries.
6. Rhythm and emotional payoff in the assembled cut.

Include timestamps and an observed reason. “Prompt followed” and “render completed” are not visual findings. If no playback/pixel/audio access exists, label the creative review unperformed and do not certify quality.
